    Data Visualizations & Analysis

    24-Hour Diurnal LAeq Profile

    What it is: The energy-average LAeq for each hour of the day (00:00–23:00), averaged across all monitoring days in the dataset, with the WHO night window (23:00–07:00) shaded and WHO 2018 road-traffic reference lines (53 dB Lden, 45 dB Lnight). How it is calculated: Each hourly point is the logarithmic energy average (LAeq) of all measurements taken during that hour across every recording day — not a simple arithmetic mean. How to read it: Peaks show the hours at which levels are consistently highest; what produces them cannot be determined from level data alone. The 45 dB line is drawn for orientation and is the WHO Lnight guideline value, which applies to the average across the whole 23:00–07:00 period rather than to any single hour. The filled area shows the level profile across the day.

    Diurnal Box-and-Whisker — Hourly LEQ Volatility

    What it is: Statistical spread of LEQ noise levels for each clock hour (00:00–23:00), pooled across all measurement days in the dataset. How it is calculated: The box spans the interquartile range (IQR: 25th–75th percentile); the centre line is the median (L50); whiskers extend to 1.5 × IQR; individual dots beyond whiskers are statistical outliers (e.g., isolated events such as sirens). How to read it: A tall box at a given hour means levels at that hour vary widely between days; a short box means they are consistent. The line inside each box is the median and the box spans the interquartile range (IQR), the middle 50% of readings. A consistently high median through 23:00–07:00 indicates raised night levels; whether the WHO Lnight guideline is met is determined in the Compliance section, which evaluates the night-period average rather than individual hours.

    Temporal Heatmap — Date × Hour of Day

    What it is: A colour-coded matrix showing LAeq noise intensity for every hour of every calendar day in the dataset. Each cell represents one hour on one date. How it is calculated: Each cell value is the logarithmic energy average (LAeq) of all measurements within that hour — never an arithmetic mean. Colour scale: green (quiet, <55 dB) → yellow (moderate, 55–65 dB) → orange (high, 65–75 dB) → red (critical, >75 dB). How to read it: Scan down a column to see how one day compares across hours. Scan across a row to see how one hour behaves day-over-day. A consistently red/orange row at 07:00–09:00 indicates a chronic morning traffic peak. Isolated red cells flag one-off events.

    Noise Exceedance Percentiles — L5, L10, L50, L90, L95

    What it is: The five standard acoustic exceedance levels showing the dB(A) value exceeded for a given fraction of the monitoring period. How it is calculated: Lₓ is the level exceeded x% of the total monitoring time. L5 = loudest 5% (acute events). L50 = median acoustic climate. L90 = statistical background floor (the level the site is noisier than 90% of the time). How to read it: A wide L10–L90 gap (>15 dB) signals a highly variable, event-driven environment. If L90 alone exceeds 45 dB(A), persistent background noise is the primary health concern — not just peaks.

    Daily Acoustic Trend — 24-hr LAeq & Nighttime LAeq

    What it is: Day-by-day acoustic trend showing the 24-hr energy-average LAeq and the nighttime LAeq (22:00–07:00) for each calendar day in the monitoring period. The dashed 53 dB and 45 dB lines are drawn for visual orientation only: they are the WHO 2018 Lden and Lnight guideline values, and those guidelines apply to penalty-weighted long-term averages, not to a single day's LAeq. A day's trace rising above a line is therefore not an exceedance — the compliance verdict is given in the Compliance section, which evaluates Lden and Lnight directly. How it is calculated: Each daily point is the logarithmic energy average (LAeq) of all measurements within that calendar day. Nighttime points use only samples between 22:00–07:00. WHO reference lines are drawn at 53 dB(A) (Lden limit) and 45 dB(A) (Lnight limit). How to read it: Compare days against each other to find the loudest and quietest in the record, and compare the two traces to see how far each day's level falls after dark. The dashed lines are fixed reference levels, not per-day limits: a point above one is a louder day, not a guideline exceedance.

    Standards & Health Compliance — Pass/Fail Matrix

    Standards sourced exclusively from WHO Environmental Noise Guidelines (2018), WHO Guidelines for Community Noise (1999), and Maryland COMAR 26.02.03.02. OSHA/NIOSH occupational limits are listed separately in the Standards tab — they apply to workplaces, not residential or community environments.

    How to read: A green PASS means your measured level is at or below the health/legal limit. A red FAIL means the limit is exceeded by the number of dB shown in the Delta column. Hover the ⓘ icon for the clinical basis behind each standard.

      Methodological Limitations & Disclaimer

      Source Attribution

      Acoustic sensors measure total environmental energy and cannot definitively identify specific noise sources. Source-specific compliance requires cross-referencing with external databases (e.g., ADS-B flight tracking).

      Health vs. Legal Limits

      This platform evaluates data against WHO 2018 biological health guidelines and local regulatory limits (e.g., Maryland COMAR). Passing local zoning limits does not guarantee absence of adverse health impacts.

      Equipment Calibration

      Accuracy of these metrics depends on proper sensor deployment, windshielding, and recent acoustic calibration of the monitoring hardware.

      Not Medical Advice

      This data is for environmental and epidemiological research only. It does not constitute a clinical medical diagnosis or formal legal counsel.
